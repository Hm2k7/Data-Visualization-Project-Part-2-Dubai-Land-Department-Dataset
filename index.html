Task 1: Transactions by Day of the Week

Person 1: Task 1 + Data Setup

Number of transactions registered on each day of the week across the analysis window. Dates are read as UTC, so the counts are the same in every time zone, and Saturday and Sunday stay on the axis even though they have no transactions.

Task 1 Findings & Explanation

Answer: 6,553 Friday Transactions

Time Zone Integrity (Why UTC is Required): Dates are parsed explicitly as UTC (format utc:'%Y-%m-%d'), the weekday is taken with utcday(datum.instance_date), and the analysis window is filtered with utc() date bounds. If local time were used instead, a viewer west of Greenwich (such as the Americas) would see every date pushed back into the previous calendar day: Friday transactions would appear under Thursday, and the window would pick up the transactions of 1 September 2026. Using UTC throughout means every viewer sees identical numbers regardless of their location or computer settings.

Historical Policy & Weekend Registrations: Saturday and Sunday display 0 transactions because DLD registration authorities do not record transactions during weekend closures. Furthermore, as identified in our Part 1 Tableau analysis, Friday volume was minimal prior to 2022 (only 299 transactions from 2015–2021) and surged from 2022 onwards (6,254 transactions) when the UAE transitioned its official weekend from Friday–Saturday to Saturday–Sunday, making Friday a half working day.

Task 2: Monthly Sales Trends (Off-Plan vs. Existing Properties)

Person 2: Task 2 + Page & Hosting

Monthly property sales volume from January 2015 to August 2026, comparing off-plan developments against existing (ready) properties with peak annotation and interactive multi-series tooltips.

Task 2 Findings & Technical Decisions

Answer: September 2025 recorded the highest off-plan sales with 706 sales

Design & Analytical Justifications:

  • Why a Line Chart Suits Monthly Data: Time is a continuous, sequential dimension. A multi-series line chart effectively highlights trajectories, rates of change, cyclical seasonal slowdowns, and the dramatic divergence between ready and off-plan sales post-2021. In contrast, representing 140 months across 2 series with bar charts would create 280 cluttered bars, making long-term market trends difficult to perceive.
  • Why the X-Axis Domain is Fixed (Jan 2015 – Aug 2026): Explicitly fixing the x-axis scale across all 140 months provides an unbroken chronological baseline. It prevents temporal compression, avoids artificial gaps, aligns with the shared project window, and faithfully represents quieter market periods (such as the April 2020 COVID lockdown trough) alongside peak recovery periods.
  • Connection to Part 1 (Tableau) Insights: In Part 1, aggregate monthly transactions (including mortgages and commercial deals) peaked in July 2025 at 1,307 transactions. By refining the analysis in Part 2 specifically to sales transactions and separating off-plan from ready properties, we uncover that off-plan sales reached their individual apex two months later, in September 2025 with 706 sales, illustrating how developer off-plan launches propelled late-2025 transaction velocity.
  • Interactive Multi-Series Tooltip: Implementing a pivot transform by registration type coupled with a vertical rule and nearest selection allows users to hover anywhere on the canvas to inspect synchronized values for both Off-Plan and Existing sales for any given month.

Task 3: Map of Prices Across Dubai's Communities

Person 3: Task 3 (Map)

Median price per square metre of unit and villa sales in each Dubai Municipality community. Hover over a community to see its name, number of sales and median price.

Task 3 Findings & Explanation

Answer: Al Barsha South Fourth (Jumeirah Village Circle), with 3,925 unit & villa sales

Al Barsha South Fourth (community 681, the community that contains Jumeirah Village Circle) has the largest number of unit and villa sales in the analysis window: 3,925 sales, at a median of 12,923 AED/m². It is followed by Business Bay (3,634) and Marsa Dubai, i.e. Dubai Marina (2,955).

Is the map effective for finding this community? No. The map encodes the median price with colour. The number of sales is not shown by any visual channel, only in the tooltip, so to find the busiest community you would have to hover over every coloured community and remember the counts. The map also draws the eye the wrong way: the size of a polygon shows land area, not activity, so the big inland communities stand out even though most of them have no sales. Al Barsha South Fourth, by contrast, is an average-sized 6.8 km² community in a middle colour class. By the effectiveness principle, a quantity that has to be compared precisely should be shown with position or length on a common scale. A bar chart of communities sorted by number of sales would find it immediately. The map answers "where is housing expensive?", not "where are most sales?".

Design & Analytical Justifications:

  • Starting point: adapted from the Vega-Lite gallery choropleths. The transactions are aggregated per community, and each community's shape is joined with a lookup on comm_num = COMM_NUM and drawn as a geoshape.
  • Communities with only a few sales: a median of a handful of sales is unreliable, because one villa can set it. Communities with fewer than 10 sales (37 of them) are therefore not coloured and stay grey.
  • Colour scale chosen for how prices are distributed: the medians are skewed, ranging from 3,223 to 75,635 AED/m² but clustering around 15,000. On a linear scale almost every community would get the same light colour. A threshold scale with round breaks near the quintiles (11,000 / 14,000 / 18,000 / 25,000 AED/m²) puts 14–16 of the 76 coloured communities in each of the five colours, so every colour is used. A single-hue blue ramp shows order: light = cheaper, dark = more expensive.
  • Every community is visible: a first layer draws all 226 community shapes from the GeoJSON in grey, and the coloured communities are drawn on top. Grey means no unit or villa sales, or fewer than 10.
  • Projection & framing: Mercator (conformal, so community shapes are not distorted, as on everyday web maps), fitted automatically to the community boundaries so the whole emirate is shown with nothing cut off. The map uses the full width of the page so the city is drawn as large as possible.
  • Tooltip: community name, number of unit and villa sales, and median price.
